  ============================================================
  BEFORE THE VIDEO
  ============================================================

  [ ] Hand each kid worksheet-1 (self-quiz) ONLY
      Do NOT give them worksheet-2 or 3 yet — they will read ahead
      and it ruins the big question at the end.

  [ ] Say: "Write your own answers. No looking at your brother's
      paper. These are private — I will not share them with anyone."

  [ ] Press play

  ============================================================
  0:00-0:40 — INTRO (no pause needed)
  ============================================================

  Key stat: By age 12, you have spent about an entire year online.

  ============================================================
  0:41 — PAUSE: SELF-QUIZ
  ============================================================

  [ ] Kids fill out worksheet-1 (self-quiz) — 6 yes/no questions

  Scoring: More than 3 "yes" = screen time might be causing problems

  After both are done, discuss together:
  - "How did you score? Be honest — I'm not going to be mad."
  - "Which questions were the easiest to say yes to?"
  - "Do you think the quiz is fair, or rigged to make everyone
    look addicted?"

  [ ] Take the quiz yourself. Show your own score. This models
      honesty and removes the "you're just lecturing me" dynamic.

  Listen for: Honest answers are the win, not low scores. If they
  score 5-6 and are honest about it, that is self-awareness.

  ============================================================
  2:26 — PAUSE: HAND OUT WORKSHEET-2
  ============================================================

  [ ] Give each kid worksheet-2 (video notes)
      They write down things that surprise them during each chapter.

  [ ] Press play

  ============================================================
  4:56 — PAUSE: DISCUSS (Intermittent Rewards)
  ============================================================

  Key concept: Slot machine mechanism — unpredictable rewards keep
  you hooked. Every 3rd, 7th, or 2nd scroll gives something good.
  Surprise itself triggers bigger dopamine (the puppy example).

  Questions:
  - "She shows a puppy as a surprise. Why did a surprise puppy
    give you more dopamine than if I told you 'look at this puppy'
    and showed you one?"
  - "How is scrolling like a slot machine? What is the 'reward'
    when you scroll?"
  - "She says rewards come every 3rd, 7th, or 2nd time —
    unpredictable. Why is unpredictable better than predictable
    for keeping you hooked?"

  Try this: "If YouTube showed you only one good video every 20
  videos, and told you which one it was, would you watch the other
  19?"

  Listen for: Can they articulate "I keep scrolling because I don't
  know when something good will show up"? If yes, they get it.

  [ ] Press play

  ============================================================
  7:48 — PAUSE: DISCUSS (Long-Term Impacts)
  ============================================================

  Key concept: Cutting from 3 hours to 1 hour improves self-esteem,
  sleep, and focus in 21 days. First few days feel like "melting
  from boredom" but it gets better.

  Questions:
  - "She says cutting from 3 hours to 1 hour can improve how you
    feel in 3 weeks. Does 3 weeks feel long or short?"
  - "She says the first few days feel like 'melting from boredom.'
    Why does that happen? What is your brain doing during that
    boredom?"
  - "Has anyone ever told you boredom is bad? Do you think boredom
    can actually be useful?"

  Listen for: Can they connect boredom to their brain resetting?
  If they think "boredom is just bad," redirect to: "What if
  boredom is your brain's way of processing and resetting?"

  [ ] Press play

  ============================================================
  9:15 — PAUSE: DISCUSS (Seven Level Ups)
  ============================================================

  Key concept: Seven practical strategies, framed as "level ups."

  The 7 level ups:
  1. No screens in the bedroom
  2. Try screen-free mornings
  3. Set your phone screen to grayscale
  4. Hide your phone out of sight
  5. Plan screen-free hangouts
  6. Use screens better (connect, hobbies, learn — not just scroll)
  7. Turn off notifications on social media and gaming apps

  Questions:
  - "Which of these level ups do you think would be the hardest
    for you? Why?"
  - "Which ones do you already do?"
  - "She says you don't have to use screens less — just use them
    better. What does that mean? Can you give an example of
    'better' vs 'worse' screen use?"

  Highlight: Grayscale makes the tablet less visually stimulating,
  which reduces the dopamine trigger. It is a concrete, reversible
  experiment they can try immediately.

  [ ] Press play — watch to the end (13:21)

  ============================================================
  VIDEO ENDS — COLLECT WORKSHEETS
  ============================================================

  [ ] Collect worksheet-1 and worksheet-2 from both kids
  [ ] Do NOT review them yet — review after the session

  ============================================================
  REFLECTION — HAND OUT WORKSHEET-3
  ============================================================

  [ ] Give each kid worksheet-3 (level ups + big question)
  [ ] They fill it out privately — no sharing, no looking at
      brother's paper

  This worksheet asks them to:
  - Pick 2 level ups they want to try this week
  - Propose their daily screen time number (hours/day)
  - Answer: "Are you the boss of your tablet or is it the boss
    of you?"

  [ ] Wait until both are done

  ============================================================
  ONE-ON-ONE CONVERSATION (separate, ~5 min each)
  ============================================================

  [ ] Take each boy aside INDIVIDUALLY (not together — twins
      will mirror each other)

  Ask:
  - "Are you the boss of your tablet or is it the boss of you?"
  - Write down what each says
  - Do not correct or judge — just listen

  This conversation is more valuable than the written answer.
  The worksheet captures their private reflection; this is where
  the real self-awareness surfaces.

  ============================================================
  SESSION END
  ============================================================

  You now have:
  - 2 self-quizzes (worksheet-1) — their self-assessment
  - 2 video notes (worksheet-2) — what caught their attention
  - 2 level-up picks + proposed numbers (worksheet-3)
  - 2 "boss of your tablet" conversations — verbal + written

  NEXT STEP: Phase 5 — Family Plan (separate session, ~30-45 min)
  Bring both kids together and fill out family-plan-agreement.txt
  using the numbers they each proposed. See parent-guides/
  04-family-plan-guide.md for how to negotiate without overriding
