**Purpose:** Low-stakes comprehension check. Not for a grade — it is to see which concepts stuck and which need another conversation.

## How to Use This Quiz

Give this AFTER watching the video together and having the discussion. Frame it as: "Let's see what you remember — this is not for a grade, it's to see what made sense and what we should talk about more."

Answer key is at the bottom. If they miss a question, that tells you which concept to revisit.

## Quiz: Multiple Choice

**1. Nicole says that by the time you are 12, you have probably spent:**
   a) One month of your life online
   b) Six months of your life online
   c) An entire year of your life online
   d) Two years of your life online

**2. Nicole says addiction means:**
   a) Liking something a lot
   b) Doing something so much it causes negative effects but you still can't stop
   c) Doing something every day
   d) Wanting to do something

**3. The self-quiz in the video has 6 questions. How many "yes" answers mean your screen time might be causing problems?**
   a) More than 1
   b) More than 2
   c) More than 3
   d) All 6

**4. Dopamine is described as:**
   a) A brain chemical that makes you feel tired
   b) A brain chemical that acts like a reward — it feels good when you find food, solve a problem, or see something you like
   c) A brain chemical that only comes from drugs
   d) A brain chemical that helps you sleep

**5. Nicole says scrolling gives you lots of dopamine because:**
   a) Scrolling is exercise for your fingers
   b) Every scroll shows you something new, and each new thing gives your brain a little burst of dopamine
   c) Scrolling releases adrenaline instead of dopamine
   d) Scrolling does not actually give you dopamine

**6. What does Nicole say about adrenaline and screens?**
   a) Screens do not trigger adrenaline
   b) Our prehistoric ancestors burned off adrenaline by fighting or running, but when we get it from screens we just sit there
   c) Adrenaline from screens is weaker than from real life
   d) Adrenaline only comes from scary games

**7. What is "intermittent reinforcement"?**
   a) Getting a reward every single time you do something
   b) Never getting a reward
   c) Getting rewards at unpredictable times — sometimes every 3rd, sometimes every 7th — so you never know when the next good thing is coming
   d) Getting a reward only once

**8. Nicole compares scrolling to what other activity that uses intermittent reinforcement?**
   a) Playing sports
   b) Reading books
   c) A slot machine in a casino
   d) Watching TV

**9. Nicole says that when a puppy appeared as a surprise, your brain released MORE dopamine than if you knew it was coming. Why?**
   a) Because puppies are cuter than other animals
   b) Because surprise itself triggers a bigger dopamine release
   c) Because puppies smell good
   d) Because you were not expecting to see any image at all

**10. Nicole says cutting screen time from 3 hours to 1 hour a day can improve self-esteem, sleep, and focus in:**
   a) 3 days
   b) 7 days
   c) 21 days (3 weeks)
   d) 3 months

**11. Nicole says the first few days of cutting back feel like:**
   a) Nothing changes
   b) You are going to "melt from boredom"
   c) You feel immediately better
   d) You feel angry at everyone

**12. Which of these is NOT one of the 7 "level ups" Nicole suggests?**
   a) No screens in the bedroom
   b) Set your phone screen to grayscale
   c) Delete all your apps
   d) Turn off notifications on social media and gaming apps

**13. Nicole says you don't have to just use screens LESS — you can also use them:**
   a) Faster
   b) Better — to connect with people, practice hobbies, and learn skills
   c) On a bigger screen
   d) Only on weekends

## Short Answer

**14. In your own words, explain why scrolling is like a slot machine. Why does the unpredictability keep you hooked?** (2-3 sentences)

**15. Name THREE of the 7 "level ups" Nicole suggests for taking control of your screen time.**

**16. Nicole says "you can become the boss of your phone." What do you think she means by that? Do you feel like you are the boss of your tablet right now, or is it the boss of you? Be honest.**

---

## Answer Key

1. **c** — An entire year of your life online
2. **b** — Doing something so much it causes negative effects but you still can't stop
3. **c** — More than 3 out of 6
4. **b** — A brain chemical that acts like a reward for things that help you survive
5. **b** — Every scroll shows something new, giving a little dopamine burst
6. **b** — Prehistoric ancestors burned it off by fighting or running; we just sit there
7. **c** — Rewards at unpredictable times so you never know when the next one is coming
8. **c** — A slot machine in a casino
9. **b** — Surprise itself triggers a bigger dopamine release
10. **c** — 21 days (3 weeks)
11. **b** — "Melt from boredom"
12. **c** — Delete all your apps (this is NOT one of the 7 level ups)
13. **b** — Better — connect with people, practice hobbies, learn skills

**Short answer guidance (not rigid answers — look for understanding):**

14. Look for: the reward is unpredictable / you don't know when something good will show up / that unpredictability keeps you scrolling because the next one might be the good one / predictable rewards get boring, unpredictable ones keep you hooked. If they mention the slot machine comparison, that counts.

15. Any three of: no screens in bedroom / screen-free mornings / grayscale / hide phone out of sight / plan screen-free hangouts / use screens better (connect, hobbies, learn) / turn off notifications.

16. This is the most important question. There is no wrong answer. Look for honesty and self-awareness. If they say "my tablet is the boss of me" — that is self-awareness, not failure. If they say "I am the boss" — ask them to give an example of a time they chose to stop. The goal is honest reflection, not the "right" answer. This question is where the self-regulation goal actually lives.
