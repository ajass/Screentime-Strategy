# Screentime Strategy

Resources for teaching 11-year-old kids about screen time, dopamine, and self-regulation.

## Contents

- **screen-time.md** — Research-backed guide for parents: what the science says about screen time effects, how to explain it to kids, and strategies for teaching self-regulation
- **screen-time-video-discussion.md** — Discussion guide for watching "Solving Screen Addiction for Kids & Teens" (Media Stamped / Global Citizen) together. Pauses at chapter markers with conversation questions. Includes parent approach for twins.
- **screen-time-video-quiz.md** — Low-stakes comprehension quiz (13 questions) to check understanding after watching the video. Includes parent administration instructions for twins.
- **worksheet-part1-self-quiz.txt** — Printable. Handed out BEFORE the video. Contains the 6-question self-quiz only.
- **worksheet-part2-notes.txt** — Printable. Handed out at 2:26 during the video. Contains note-taking space for each chapter.
- **worksheet-part3-levelups-bigquestion.txt** — Printable. Handed out AFTER the video. Contains level-up picks and the big question about reasonable screen time.

## Approach

The goal is not to control or ban screens. It is to teach kids WHY excessive screen time is harmful so they develop self-regulation skills. They need to understand the reasoning, not just follow rules.

Designed for twin 11-year-old boys. The worksheet is split into three parts to prevent reading ahead, which contaminates honest answers.

## Deployment Instructions

### Before You Start

1. Print these files (2 copies each — one per kid):
   - worksheet-part1-self-quiz.txt
   - worksheet-part2-notes.txt
   - worksheet-part3-levelups-bigquestion.txt
   - screen-time-video-quiz.md (the quiz questions only, pages 2-3 — do NOT print the answer key)

2. Read screen-time-video-discussion.md and screen-time-video-quiz.md fully so you know the parent approach and what to listen for.

3. Set up the video on your TV or shared screen: https://youtu.be/6iJdRoeYay0

4. Do NOT hand out all worksheets at once. 11-year-olds will read ahead, which ruins the self-quiz and the big question. Hand them out one at a time as described below.

### Step-by-Step Flow

**Before pressing play:**
- Hand each boy Part 1 (self-quiz) only
- Tell them: "Write your own answers. No looking at your brother's paper. These are private — I will not share them with anyone."
- Do NOT give them Parts 2 or 3 yet

**Press play. Watch together.**

**At 0:41 (self-quiz chapter):**
- Pause the video
- Let them fill out Part 1
- Do not review or discuss their answers yet — collect them privately

**Resume play.** Discuss the chapter questions from the discussion guide out loud together at each chapter marker.

**At 2:26 (dopamine & adrenaline chapter):**
- Pause
- Hand each boy Part 2 (notes)
- Resume — they take notes during each remaining chapter

**Continue pausing at each chapter (4:56, 7:48, 9:15) for discussion questions.**

**Video ends.** Collect Part 2 notes. Do not review yet.

**After the video — the quiz:**
- Send one boy to another room
- Give the first boy the printed quiz (questions only, no answer key)
- Let him complete it on paper, no screens
- Then swap — second boy takes the quiz in a separate room

**After the quiz — Part 3 (level ups + big question):**
- Hand each boy Part 3 privately
- Let them fill it out on their own, not side by side
- Collect when done

**The one-on-one conversation:**
- Take each boy aside individually (not together)
- Ask: "Do you feel like you are the boss of your tablet, or is it the boss of you?"
- This question gets a very different answer one-on-one vs. on the couch next to a sibling
- Write down what each says

**Bring them back together:**
- You now have two independent answers for reasonable screen time
- Say: "You each came up with your own number. Let's talk about how we combine these into a family plan."
- Build the agreement together based on their independent input

### Timing

Total time: approximately 60-75 minutes
- Video + discussion: 25-30 min
- Quiz (two boys separately): 15 min
- Part 3 worksheets: 10 min
- One-on-one conversations: 10 min
- Group agreement discussion: 10-15 min
